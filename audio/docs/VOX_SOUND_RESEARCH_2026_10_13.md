# Vox sound research and channel selection brief

Research date: 13 October 2026. Status: source-backed production research plus original channel audition criteria. No new sounds have been downloaded or listening-approved by this research pass.

## What the evidence establishes

1. In a 2020 Open Notebook interview about a 2019 black-hole video, Joss Fong describes choosing minimally arranged, energetic music; editing sections and beats; and placing starts/endings around explanatory points. She warns against arrangements that compete with narration. She names APM as Vox Media's music source at that time. This is historical testimony, not verification of their 2026 supplier or a free license.
2. In a Uses This interview dated December 2018, Estelle Caswell describes substantial audio-editing time and building music playlists during outlining. She also describes composing some Earworm music with an OP-1. The lesson is planning sound during editorial development.
3. Caswell's School of Motion interview describes manually animating to the beats/syllables of rap, making sound visually understandable, and prioritizing evidence in editorial video. Earworm is a music-analysis series; its musical demonstrations should not be treated as a universal SFX recipe.
4. Soundsnap's current site lists Vox Media among organizations using its service. That is a provider's customer claim; it identifies neither individual Vox YouTube films nor the paper/marker samples we want. It does not justify saying that every Vox video uses Soundsnap.
5. Premiere Gal's own 2021 paper-highlighter tutorial recommends marker effects selected to fit the animation, layered and aligned with the visuals, then adjusted during playback. This is a third-party tutorial tagged Vox, not a Vox production manual.

## Consequence for our production

Select sounds by the explanatory action, material, envelope and mix context. Plan score changes alongside the visual argument. A collection of paper noises alone will not establish editorial rhythm.

The following targets are our creative adaptations for the channel, not measured proprietary Vox settings. They are intended to guide the upcoming free-asset audition.

| Visible action | Candidate character and searches | Selection test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Article slides into frame | Close, dry sheet friction; paper slide, document handling | The scrape follows travel; a soft contact resolves the landing |
| Page turns or flips past camera | Flexible paper flutter; single page turn, paper flick | Air and flutter match scale/speed without a metallic or bass-heavy tail |
| Yellow highlight grows | Textured felt-tip drag; highlighter stroke, marker on paper | Sustained friction covers the stroke; tiny contact/release accents define its ends |
| Route or handwritten arrow draws | Fine, grainy scratching; pencil writing, pen sketching | Motion and sound start together, pause together and finish together |
| Photo is placed or swapped | Light cardstock tap plus handling; photograph handling, card on desk | Weight is credible without a heavy cinematic hit |
| Camera moves between graphic states | Short, restrained air movement; soft swish, cloth swish | Supports a meaningful reframe and clears the next word |
| Source detail snaps into attention | Brief mechanical punctuation; camera shutter, small switch, click | Clear at phone volume, with no sharp resonance masking consonants |
| Information locks into place | Small physical tap/click; wood tap, button click | Marks a decision or reveal without implying a real mechanism that isn't there |
| Film mechanism / archival atmosphere | Low, irregular mechanical texture; film projector, reel mechanism | Supports the material illusion at low level; does not fight every transient |
| Explanatory turn or payoff | Edited musical cadence, pause or arrangement change | Marks a new understanding and leaves the key narration intelligible |

Begin with dry, close recordings: they are easier to shape and place. Audition short, medium and long variants rather than stretching one clip across every motion. Avoid leading silence, intrusive room noise, clipped peaks or excessively long tails unless the scene benefits from them. Do not assert recording authenticity from filenames.

## Timing and mixing direction

Our starting rules:
- Define the animation event first: contact, launch, maximum travel, reveal, settle or release.
- Shape the sound to that event's envelope. For a highlight, prefer contact → drag → release. For a page arrival, prefer friction → light landing.
- Preserve distinctive layers: voice, music, ambience, physical Foley and editorial punctuation. Listen to the combined mix.
- Keep physical actions audible, as the owner requested. Subtle continuous motion can share a material sound bed instead of retriggering identical accents.
- Keep source-local movement spatially plausible. Use restrained panning and test mono playback on a phone.
- Build score sections around hook, context, mechanism, contradiction and payoff. End or simplify music at important deductions, then introduce the next section deliberately.
- Keep film grain/jitter an aesthetic treatment unless a motivated mechanism needs sound. Do not add a camera click on every grain update or line-boil frame.
- Final loudness and decode checks remain separate from aesthetic approval.

## Candidate audition and selection

1. Review existing first-party/generated/cleared recordings against this brief before downloading replacements.
2. Shortlist three candidates per missing action. Retain the original source and license for each.
3. Compare at approximately matched perceived loudness so the loudest candidate does not win automatically.
4. Audition against the same existing 12.8-second test with narration and score present. Solo playback is only an initial screen.
5. Score material fit, envelope fit, voice clarity, character/variation and recording cleanliness from 1–5. Require license eligibility separately; a sonic score cannot override missing permission.
6. Save selected originals and edited derivatives with source URLs, creator, license/version, attribution, download date, hashes, trim/gain notes and intended events.
7. Owner listening review selects the production set. Do not label a shortlist “Vox exact match” or “sound-approved” before comparison.

## Reference listening still needed

Public interviews establish editorial methods; they do not supply isolated stems or exact sample identities. For a stronger sound match, use authentic Vox examples from the retained reference set and log only authored graphic moments: action timestamp, sound onset/length, apparent material, transient/texture/tail, music change, voice overlap and uncertainty.

Do not identify a paper sound merely because paper appears. Listen to the transition and the surrounding mix. Do not reuse extracted reference audio in our film or sound library. Do not report timecoded listening analysis as completed until it has actually been performed.

## Free-asset licensing route

Prefer individually checked CC0 recordings where available; CC BY requires recorded attribution. Mixkit's SFX license permits incorporation into commercial videos but prohibits standalone redistribution and inclusion with source files. Keep restricted stock out of public GitHub and distributed rebuild bundles. Preserve a public metadata index and fetch instructions when the raw files cannot be redistributed.
